Protocol Flow

Blind Sharded Issuance: ownership is generated only when custody, audit, validation, and settlement conditions are completed correctly.

1. Buyer Order

The buyer places an order, for example 1 kg of gold. Buyer funds are locked in escrow and cannot be released until validation is complete.

↓

2. Order Fragmentation

The protocol fragments the order into independent execution shards. Each shard represents only a partial quantity of the total order.

Shard #1 — 50g
Shard #2 — 30g
Shard #3 — 120g
Shard #n — ...
↓

3. Demand / Supply Matching

Each purchase shard is deterministically matched with corresponding supply-side shards. Every acquisition corresponds to a verified sale or delivery obligation.

↓

4. Probabilistic Role Distribution

For each shard, the protocol assigns execution responsibilities across different actors. No single participant receives full visibility over the complete order.

Issuer Function Executes assigned acquisition logic and commits capital exposure.
Vault Receives and records the physical asset for its assigned shard.
Auditor Set Validates shard-specific custody and real-world event consistency.
↓

5. Physical Transfer

Physical Reserve Layer Formation Physical transfer is required during the initial bootstrap phase, when verified reserves are first introduced into the system.

Each completed and validated shard contributes to the formation of a persistent physical reserve layer within vaults.

As this layer grows, an increasing share of transactions operates on already verified custody, enabling ownership transfer without physical movement.

The physical asset is moved through the logistics layer. Transport delivers the allocated gold portions to qualified vaults such as Vault 1, Vault 2, and additional vault institutions.

↓

6. Auditor Intervention

Auditors verify that the relevant real-world events occurred correctly: delivery, custody evidence, quantity, purity, and consistency with the shard record.

↓

7. Validator Intervention

Validators verify cryptographic and protocol-level conditions: signatures, registered keys, shard identifiers, non-reuse of custody records, and consistency with the protocol state.

↓

8. Protocol Reconstruction

Validated shards are reconstructed at protocol level. The full allocation is not controlled by any single participant, but emerges from verified shard completion.

↓

9. Issuance as Protocol Outcome

If all required shard conditions are valid, ownership units are generated. Issuance is not a discretionary act by an entity: it is the final protocol consequence of successful verification.

↓

10. Escrow Release

Once minting is finalized, escrowed funds are released to the corresponding sellers or shard-side liquidity providers according to protocol rules.

Clarification: in this model, the “issuer” is best understood as a protocol-bounded issuance function or execution role, not as an unconstrained central authority. Digital ownership emerges only after shard execution, real-world validation, cryptographic verification, and protocol reconstruction are completed.
