Protocol Flow

One initial order, two possible execution paths, one final condition: ownership is generated only when protocol state is valid.
Initial Order

A buyer submits an order and funds are locked in protocol-controlled escrow.

From this point, execution follows one of two paths depending on whether the required verified reserves already exist inside the Verified Reserve State (VRS).

↓
Execution Path Selection

The protocol distinguishes between VRS expansion and internal VRS-native transfer.

Bootstrap builds the VRS layer. Internal execution reallocates existing verified ownership.

Bootstrap Path

VRS Layer Construction
Used when new physical assets must enter vault custody to expand the Verified Reserve State.
1. External Issuer = Physical Seller In this path, the external issuer is not a monetary issuer. It is the seller/provider of the physical asset entering the protocol.
↓
2. Bootstrap Asset Cases The physical asset may enter the protocol through two external cases.
Case 1: the issuer buys gold externally using its own capital, assuming capital exposure until validation completes.

Case 2: the issuer already owns physical gold not yet registered in VRS and onboards it into the protocol.
↓
3. Protocol-Assigned Transport The protocol assigns transport and vault routing. Direct coordination among actors is minimized.
↓
4. Vault Entry and Audit The asset enters vault custody. Auditors verify physical existence, quantity, purity, and consistency with the custody record.
↓
5. Validator Confirmation Validators verify certificates, signatures, shard references, custody records, and protocol state consistency.
↓
6. VRS Expansion The verified asset expands the VRS layer. Buyer funds may be released only after successful validation and protocol completion.

Internal Transfer Path

VRS-Native Exchange
Used when verified reserves already exist and execution only reallocates ownership.
1. Internal Automatic Issuer Inside the protocol, issuer is not an entity. It is an automatic execution function driven by valid protocol state.
↓
2. Seller Holds VRS Ownership The seller already owns verified VRS-backed ownership and does not need to introduce new physical assets.
↓
3. Order Sharding Buyer and seller orders are decomposed into execution shards.
↓
4. Non-Linear Shard Matching Buyer shards are matched with seller shards. Matching is many-to-many, randomizable, and protocol-driven.
↓
5. Internal Acquisition Case Case 3: a participant may first enter as a buyer, acquire VRS-backed ownership, and later become a seller through ownership state transfer.
↓
6. Audit and Validation Auditors verify VRS consistency where required. Validators verify ownership state, signatures, shard integrity, and invariant compliance.
↓
7. Ownership Reallocation No physical movement occurs. Verified ownership is reassigned through protocol state transition.
Common Final Condition
I(si) = 1 ⇔ Valid(si)
Issuance is not path-dependent.
It is condition-dependent.

Both paths converge only when custody, audit, validation, and protocol invariants are satisfied.
Issuer Clarification

The protocol emits the final token or ownership unit corresponding to the validated value. The issuer does not emit currency or tokens.

In the bootstrap path, “issuer” refers to the seller/provider of the physical asset entering vault custody. In the internal path, issuer becomes an automatic protocol function coordinating shard matching and state transition.
